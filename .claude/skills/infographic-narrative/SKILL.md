# Infographic process, step 3: narrative

This skill turns the insights into candidate narratives, compares them against the outcome, and gets one chosen. The chosen narrative stays provisional until step 4 has tested it against a concept model.

## Before you start

Read `process-record.md`. You need the Brief (especially the outcome, message, audience and output type) and the Insights. If either is missing, stop and tell the person to start with the `infographic-process` skill.

Do not start another step. When you finish, report back as described at the end.

## What to do

1. **Restate the aim.** Write the outcome and the draft message at the top of the Candidate narratives heading, so every candidate is judged against the same aim.
2. **Write candidate narratives.** Write at least two and usually three. The candidates must differ in what they claim or in the order they tell it, not only in wording. This default count is a working choice and has no source behind it. Use the format below.
3. **List the assumptions.** For each candidate, name the assumptions it depends on, and the best rival explanation that would make it wrong.
4. **Compare against the outcome.** Rate each candidate high, medium or low on four questions, each with a reason: does it lead the reader to the outcome, do the insights support it, does it suit this audience, and does it suit the output type.
5. **Recommend one.** Give your recommendation and the reasons in a few sentences. Do not choose for the person.
6. **Run the horizontal logic check** on the recommended candidate, as described below.
7. **Ask the person to confirm.** Present the candidates, the comparison and the recommendation. Record the person's choice under Chosen narrative with the status "provisional", the reasons, and any changes they asked for.

## What a narrative contains for each output type

- **Types 1 and 2 (linear and annotated stories):** the full narrative, meaning the order, the wording of the action titles and the call to action.
- **Types 3 and 4 (guided and explorable):** the guided path, the transition from the guided part to exploration, the default views and the entry points.
- **Types 5 and 6 (dashboards and technical visualisations):** there is no narrative. The story becomes the questions the tool must answer, and the message becomes a purpose statement. Write candidates as sets of questions, and run the horizontal logic check on the question list.

## Candidate format

```
### C1 <short name>
- Message: one sentence
- Points in order: the action titles, one line each
- Reader ends up able to: link to the outcome
- Uses: insight ids
- Leaves out: what it does not say
- Depends on: assumptions
- Could be wrong if: the best rival explanation
- Fit: high, medium or low, with a reason for each of the four questions
```

## Horizontal logic check

An action title says what the reader should take from the part, not what the part contains. "Costs rose faster than wages" is an action title. "Costs and wages" is not.

Read only the action titles of the recommended candidate, in order, as if they were the whole work. Check that they tell the narrative from the opening to the call to action, with no missing step and no title that repeats another. If they do not, rewrite the titles or the order and run the check again. Report the result in the step report.

## Step report

Add one line to the decision log and tell the person in a few sentences.

- **Complete:** "Step 3 complete: N candidates, C<number> chosen (provisional), horizontal logic passed." The process continues to step 4.
- **Blocked:** "Step 3 blocked: <reason>", for example when the insights cannot support any candidate that leads to the outcome. The orchestrator decides what happens next.

## What this step reads and writes

- Reads: Brief, Insights.
- Writes: Candidate narratives, Chosen narrative (status provisional).
